What the payroll system does

Applies to: Avinash Group · FY 83/84 · NGI, NGN, NGG, NGKAudience: Everyone
In this articleThe chain from a fingerprint on the door to a journal entry in the books, and the three things it produces.

This system pays the staff of the Avinash Group companies. It replaces the Excel salary sheet that each company kept — not by copying it, but by building the same figures from records the system already holds: who came to work, what they are paid, what they are owed, what must be taken off.

The chain

Every rupee on a payslip can be traced back along one chain. If a figure is wrong, the fault is at one of these links, and this documentation is organised in the same order.

Punch on the device→Employee Checkin→Attendance →Payroll Adjustment→Salary Slip→Journal Entry →Bank payment
LinkWhat it holdsWho touches it
Employee CheckinOne row per punch: person, time, device, IN or OUT.Nobody — the device writes it. HR corrects only what is missing.
AttendanceOne row per person per day: Present, Absent, Half Day, On Leave, Holiday; the shift; the hours worked.Built nightly by the system. HR fixes exceptions.
Payroll AdjustmentWhat attendance is worth in money: tea, meals, overtime, late fines, for one company and one month.HR, once a month, before slips.
Salary SlipOne person, one month: every earning, every deduction, the tax, the net.Created by the Payroll Entry, checked by HR, approved by accounts.
Journal EntryThe month's cost in the books, split by section, with SSF and TDS as liabilities.Posted when the slips are submitted.

What it produces

  1. A payslip for each employee — the same figures the Excel sheet produced, to the paisa, but derived rather than typed.
  2. An entry in the accounts — salary cost against the right section, SSF and income tax held as payable, net pay held as Salary Payable until the bank pays it.
  3. A record that survives an audit — every figure has a document behind it: an attendance row, an approved overtime sheet, a signed advance.
What this system does not do

It does not decide policy. Whether someone gets 21 days of casual leave, whether the plant staff are paid overtime, what a meal is worth — those are the client's decisions, already made, and this system only carries them out. Every one of them is a setting, and this documentation says where the setting is.

The payroll month is a Nepali month

The books, the salary sheets and the staff all work in Bikram Sambat. A payroll month is a BS month, which never lines up with an English one.

BS monthStarts (AD)Ends (AD)Days
Shrawan 20832026-07-172026-08-1631
Bhadra 20832026-08-172026-09-1631
Ashwin 20832026-09-172026-10-1731
Kartik 20832026-10-182026-11-1630

A BS month is 29, 30, 31 or 32 days long. Proration therefore divides by the days in that month, never by a fixed 30. The one exception is the hourly rate behind overtime and late fines, where the Labour Act itself says to divide the monthly basic by 30 — see Calculation reference.

Important

Fiscal year 83/84 runs 2026-07-17 to 2027-07-16. Every period record in the system — Fiscal Year, Payroll Period, Leave Period, Holiday List — must use exactly those two dates. A one-day difference produces leave balances and tax projections that are almost right, which is far worse than obviously wrong.

Settings, records and results

Three kinds of thing live in the system, and knowing which is which tells you whether you may edit it.

KindExamplesRule
Setting — decided once, changed rarelyShift Type, Salary Component, Salary Structure, Leave Policy, Income Tax Slab, Holiday ListChange it deliberately, in a change window, and know that it affects every future run. Most do not affect the past.
Record — a fact about a person or a dayEmployee, Attendance, Leave Application, Overtime Sheet, Employee Advance, Salary Structure AssignmentEnter and correct freely while it is a draft. A submitted record is cancelled and replaced, never edited.
Result — worked out from the two abovePayroll Adjustment rows, Salary Slip, Journal EntryNever type over a result. If a result is wrong, the setting or the record behind it is wrong. Fix that and rebuild.
Tip

The single most common support call in a payroll system is someone who edited a result. A slip amount typed by hand looks right for one month and then never reconciles again: the journal, the tax projection and next month's figures all still come from the record behind it.

Draft, submitted, cancelled

Most payroll documents are submittable. They move through three states and the buttons change with them.

StateShown asWhat you can do
DraftRed Not Saved or grey DraftEdit anything, delete it. It has no effect on anything else yet.
SubmittedBlue SubmittedIt now counts — a submitted slip is payable, a submitted allocation is a balance. Only a few fields stay editable.
CancelledRed CancelledIt counts for nothing. It stays visible as history. To redo it, use Amend, which makes a new draft copy.

Create the year's periods

Applies to: All companies · Once a yearAudience: Implementer
In this articleFiscal Year, Payroll Period and Leave Period — three records that must agree to the day.

Three separate records describe the same year to three different parts of the system. Accounting reads the Fiscal Year, tax reads the Payroll Period, and leave balances read the Leave Period. They must carry identical dates or the three will quietly disagree.

Before you begin

  • Know the year's exact AD dates. For 83/84 that is 2026-07-17 to 2027-07-16.
  • You need the HR Manager and Accounts Manager roles.

Create the Fiscal Year

  1. Search for Fiscal Year and choose + Add Fiscal Year.
  2. Year Name: 83/84. Use the same form every year — a stray "2083/84" makes a second, competing year.
  3. Year Start Date: 2026-07-17. Year End Date: 2027-07-16.
  4. In the Companies table, add a row for every one of the seven companies.
  5. Save.
Important

All seven companies, even those not on payroll. Document numbering looks up a Fiscal Year row that spans the posting date and refuses to number an invoice if the row is missing — so an incomplete fiscal year stops sales, not just payroll.

Create the Payroll Period

  1. Search for Payroll Period › + Add.
  2. Company: one company. This record is per company, so you will repeat it.
  3. Start Date / End Date: the same two dates as the fiscal year.
  4. Save, then repeat for each company on payroll.

Why it matters: income tax is an annual figure spread over the year. The payroll period is the window the system projects across — how many months are left, how much tax has already been deducted. A period that starts on the wrong day changes everyone's monthly tax.

Create the Leave Period

  1. Search for Leave Period › + Add.
  2. From Date / To Date: the same two dates again.
  3. Company: the company.
  4. Leave Is Active ticked.
  5. Save.

Result

Three records per company, all reading 2026-07-17 to 2027-07-16. Confirm by opening each list and comparing the dates side by side.

Note

Everything here can be created for all companies at once by the year-setup routine the implementer runs; these steps are the manual equivalent, and the way to repair one company that was missed.

Build the holiday lists

Applies to: Per company · Once a yearAudience: HR Manager
In this articleTwo lists per company — the common one and the women's one — the Saturdays, and the eleven festivals of 83/84.

A holiday list does three jobs at once, which is why an error here shows up in three places. It tells attendance which days are not working days, it tells the overtime rules which work is holiday work, and it tells leave how many days a leave application actually consumes.

Important

Teej is a holiday for women only. A single list cannot express that, so each company has two: a common list and a women's list that is identical plus Teej. The women's list is attached to the women's Employee records; everyone else gets the common one.

Before you begin

  • The fiscal year must exist (step 1).
  • Have the festival dates confirmed by the client. Four of the five festivals move with the moon and cannot be calculated — they are typed from the Nepali calendar each year.

Create the common list

  1. Search for Holiday List › + Add Holiday List.
  2. Holiday List Name: NGI 83/84 — company abbreviation, then the year.
  3. From Date / To Date: the fiscal year's dates.
  4. Company: the company. (On this system the field is Company on the Holiday List; it is required, and a list saved without it cannot be attached to a shift.)
  5. Under Add Weekly Holidays, set Weekly Off to Saturday and press Add to Holidays. Around 52 rows appear.
  6. Add each festival as a row in the Holidays table: the date and a description.
  7. Save.

Create the women's list

Which list applies to a person

Three places can name a holiday list, and the first one that has a value wins:

  1. The Shift Type — which is why ours are deliberately blank.
  2. The Employee — where the women's list is set.
  3. The Company default — the common list, which is what everyone else falls through to.

So a man needs no holiday list on his Employee record at all: leaving it blank is how he inherits the company's. Only the women's records name a list.

Result

14 holiday lists on the site — two per company. Each common list has 52 Saturdays plus 10 festivals; each women's list has one more.

Tip

If a holiday is declared mid-year, add the row to the list and then re-run attendance for that day. Attendance already written stays as it was: the list is read when the day is processed, not afterwards.

Important — leave Holiday List blank

A shift resolves the holiday list as the shift's own list, or failing that the employee's, or failing that the company's default. Because the shift's list wins, filling it here silently overrides every woman's holiday list — and Teej, which exists on that list alone, disappears for all of them.

All ten shifts are correctly blank today. Leave them blank: the company default supplies the Saturdays and the festivals, and each woman's own list adds Teej on top of them.

Holiday work: a decision to make

Auto-attendance skips any day that is a holiday, so punches on a Saturday or a festival produce nothing at all — 48 Saturday punches at NGI produced no attendance, and for Plant staff that is paid overtime lost.

Ticking Mark Auto Attendance on Holidays makes those punches produce a Present row by themselves. The trade-off: a Present row on a holiday also earns a day of Tea & Conveyance, which the Excel sheet never paid. Decide it with the client per company, rather than leaving it to whoever runs the month.

Important — the cutoff-time trap

During testing this field filled itself with a clock time, and because that time sat after the shift start, 2,423 of 2,808 days turned into Half Day. The rule now ignores any cutoff at or before the shift start, and the field is cleared when a shift is created — but after you save a shift, check it. An unexplained wave of half days across a whole company is this field, every time.

Result

Ten shift types on the site. Confirm each has auto-attendance ticked and Holiday List blank. Then assign people to them — see Change someone's shift.

Cost centres and company defaults

Applies to: Per company · OnceAudience: Accounts Manager

The group's chart of accounts splits salary cost three ways — 547101 O/O (office), 547102 S/D (sales and distribution), 547103 F/P (filling plant). But a salary component maps to one account per company, so the account alone can never tell office pay from plant pay.

  3. Parent Cost Center: the company's root, e.g. Nepal Gas Udhyog Pvt. Ltd. - NGI.
  4. Leave Is Group unticked. Save.
  5. Repeat for Sales & Distribution and Filling Plant.

  2. Go to the Accounts tab, then the Payroll section.
  3. Fill the four fields below and Save.

Two account traps

1. The payroll payable account must have no Account Type. HRMS refuses an account typed "Payable" for this field. On six companies the type had to be cleared. If the field will not accept your account, open the account and blank its Account Type.

Open item

The account named "547101 O/O" is ambiguous to read on a report. Accounts may wish to rename it to "Salary — Office". That is a naming decision, not a system one.

Two small records carry two large policy decisions. Neither is optional: an employee missing either one quietly loses money on the payslip.

Employee Category — paid overtime, or leave instead

The client's rule is that plant staff are paid for extra hours, and office staff are not — they earn a day off instead. That single distinction is the Employee Category.

Important

Manage attendance

Applies to: Daily · All companiesAudience: HR User
In this articleHow a punch becomes a day, what each status means, and how to correct a day without breaking the chain.

Attendance is where most payroll errors are born, because it is the only link fed by hardware. A device that lost power, a finger that did not read, a person on site who never punched — each produces an absence nobody intended, and the absence becomes money.

How a day is decided

  1. The device writes an Employee Checkin per punch, matched to a person by Attendance Device ID.
  2. Overnight, each shift's auto-attendance reads the punches inside its window.
  3. The first punch in and the last punch out give the hours worked.
  4. The hours are compared with the shift's thresholds to give a status.
  5. Lateness is measured against the shift's start, with no grace.
  6. An Attendance record is written for the person and the day.

Overtime

Applies to: Monthly · Plant staff onlyAudience: Supervisor, HR User
In this articleWhy extra hours must be authorised on a sheet before they are paid, and how the hours become money.

Working late is not overtime. Overtime is authorised extra time, and the authorisation is a document: the Overtime Sheet. Hours that are not on an approved sheet are never paid, however clearly the punches show them.

Why

Client policy 5.1. Without it, anyone who lingered after their shift would generate a claim on the company. The punches remain the evidence; the sheet is the decision.

Who is eligible

Employee CategoryExtra hoursHoliday work
PlantPaid at 1.5× the hourly basicPaid
Officer & AdminNot paidEarns compensatory leave

Raise the month's sheet

  1. Search for Overtime Sheet › + Add.
  2. Company and the month (the BS month the hours belong to).
  3. Press Get Employees — eligible staff with measured extra hours are listed with the hours the punches support.
  4. The supervisor reviews each line and edits the hours down where the time was not authorised. Do not edit them up beyond what the punches show.
  5. Save, then Submit. Submitting is the authorisation.

How the hours become money

Hours are rounded to the nearest half hour, then:

hourly basic = Basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8
overtime pay = hours × hourly basic × 1.5

Worked example — basic 19,260, three hours:

StepFigure
19,260 ÷ 30642.00 a day
642 ÷ 880.25 an hour
× 1.5120.38 an overtime hour
× 3 hours361.14

The divisor is 30, not the days in the BS month: that is what the Labour Act specifies, and it is deliberately different from how monthly pay is prorated.

Result

When the month's Payroll Adjustment is computed, each approved sheet line becomes an Overtime row. Nothing appears for an unsubmitted sheet.

Important

The sheet must be submitted before the Payroll Adjustment is computed. If a sheet is approved late, recompute the adjustment for that company and month.

Employee advances

Applies to: As requiredAudience: HR User, Accounts
In this articleLending money and recovering it from pay, in instalments, without letting the balance drift.

An advance is a loan: the company pays money now and takes it back from later payslips. Two things must stay true — the payslip deduction must stop when the loan is repaid, and the outstanding balance must be visible at any moment.

Record an advance

  1. Search for Employee Advance › + Add.
  2. Employee, Company, Purpose.
  3. Advance Amount — the total lent.
  4. Monthly Instalment — how much to recover each month.
  5. Advance Account — the company's Staff Advance account.
  6. Save, Submit, then have it approved and paid.

How recovery works

  1. When the month's adjustments are computed, every submitted advance with an outstanding balance is examined.
  2. The amount recovered is the smaller of the monthly instalment and what is still outstanding.
  3. A Salary Advance deduction row is written, carrying a reference to the advance it belongs to.
  4. When the slip is submitted, the advance's outstanding balance falls by that amount.
  5. When the balance reaches zero, no further deduction is produced.
Why the smaller of the two

A final instalment of 1,200 against an outstanding 800 would otherwise deduct 1,200 and leave the company owing 400 — a negative loan that nobody notices until the employee leaves.

Change or stop a recovery

You want to…Do this
Change the instalmentEdit Monthly Instalment on the advance. It takes effect from the next computation.
Pause recovery for a monthSet the instalment to 0, recompute the adjustment, then set it back afterwards.
Write the advance offAccounts' decision. Journal it out; do not delete the advance.
Recover a different amount onceEdit the amount on the Payroll Adjustment row before slips are created.
Account type

The Staff Advance account must be typed Receivable. On six companies it is typed Payable, which posts recovery the wrong way round. Accounts must change this before the first live advance.

Verify

Open the Employee Advance and read Paid Amount and the outstanding balance after a slip is submitted. They should move by exactly the deduction on that payslip.

Dashain bonus

Applies to: Once a year · Before DashainAudience: HR Manager
In this articleOne month's pay at Dashain, prorated for anyone who has not served the full year.

The Dashain bonus is a festival payment of one month's salary. Someone who has served the whole year gets 100% of a month; someone who joined part-way through gets the same proportion of it as the part of the year they served. Whether it is given at all is a decision the company makes each year, so the run carries a flag rather than assuming it.

Run the bonus

  1. Search for Dashain Bonus › + Add.
  2. Company and Fiscal Year = 83/84.
  3. Tick Bonus Given. Left unticked, the run records the decision not to pay and produces nothing.
  4. Payout Date — a date inside the BS month the bonus is paid in.
  5. Press Get Employees. Everyone active on a salary is listed with their months served and their computed bonus.
  6. Review the lines. Adjust an individual amount if the management has decided differently for that person, and write why in the note.
  7. Save, then Submit.

Submitting creates one Additional Salary per person, on the Dashain Bonus component, for the payout date. They appear on that month's payslips.

How the proration works

JoinedServedBonus
Before the year started12 months100% of one month
At the start of Kartik (month 4)9 months9 ÷ 12 = 75%
Mid-Poush~5.5 months~46%

"One month's salary" means the monthly figure the person is on at the time of the run — the same base the payslip uses, not the previous month's net.

Tax

The bonus is taxable, and the tax is taken in the month it is paid rather than spread over the rest of the year. That is why the generated Additional Salary carries Deduct Full Tax on Selected Payroll Date. Expect the Dashain payslip to show a noticeably larger income-tax line than other months; this is correct.

Verify

  1. Open one slip from the payout month and confirm a Dashain Bonus earning line.
  2. Compare a full-year employee's bonus with their gross — it should be one month's pay.
  3. Check a mid-year joiner's proportion against their joining date.
Tip

Cancelling the Dashain Bonus record cancels the Additional Salary records it created, provided the slips have not yet been submitted. After slips are submitted, the slips must be cancelled first.

Salary revision and arrears

Applies to: As decidedAudience: HR Manager
In this articleA dated pay rise for many people at once, with back-pay for the months already paid at the old rate.

Pay rises arrive late. The rise runs from Shrawan, the meeting happens in Mangsir, and four months have already been paid at the old rate. Two rules follow, and both are built into this document.

  1. A rise is a new dated assignment, never an edit. The old assignment stays behind as the record of what was paid before, so last year's payslips keep reconciling with last year's journals.
  2. Back-pay is arrears, paid in the month it is settled. Submitted slips are never rewritten.

Before you begin

Important

The effective date must be the first day of a BS month. A slip is paid at one rate for its whole month; a date inside a month silently takes effect the month after.

Run a revision

  1. Search for Salary Revision › + Add.
  2. Company and Effective Date — the first day of the BS month the new pay runs from.
  3. Default Increment Percent — e.g. 10. Used to fill the table; each line can be edited afterwards.
  4. Tick Pay Arrears if the back-pay is to be settled, and set Arrears Payout Date to a date in the month it will be paid.
  5. Press Get Employees. Everyone on a salary is listed with their current basic, the proposed new basic, the months already paid at the old rate, and the arrears that follow.
  6. Edit New Base per person as the management decided. Adjust the dearness allowance in the same row if it changes.
  7. Check Total Increase and Total Arrears at the bottom.
  8. Save, then Submit.

What submitting does

For each personAction
New payA new Salary Structure Assignment from the effective date, carrying the same structure, tax slab and payable account.
Dearness allowanceThe Employee field is updated; the old value is kept on the revision row as the history.
ArrearsAn Additional Salary on the Salary Arrears component, for the payout date, with full tax deducted that month.

Cancelling

Cancelling the revision cancels the assignments and the arrears it created and puts the dearness allowance back to its previous value. That is why the old value is stored on the row: the Employee field has no history of its own.

Guards

  • A person listed twice is refused.
  • A person from another company is refused.
  • A new basic of zero or less is refused.
  • A person with no current assignment is refused — there is nothing to revise.
  • A person who already has an assignment starting on that exact date is refused, so a revision cannot be run twice.

Applies to: As requiredAudience: HR User
In this articleEverything a new employee needs before their first payslip, and everything a leaver needs before their last.

A new joiner — the checklist

Do these in order. Miss one and the first payslip is wrong in a way that is hard to see.

  1. Create the Employee: name, company, date of joining, gender, status Active.
  2. Set Attendance Device ID and enrol the finger on the device.
  3. Set Default Shift and Holiday List (the women's list where it applies).
  4. Set Employee Category — Plant or Officer & Admin.
  5. Set Allowance Category if they get tea.
  6. Set Payroll Cost Center.
  7. Fill the pay inputs: initial basic, dearness, other, fuel, the gas and education ticks, the SSF tick.
  8. Create and submit a Salary Structure Assignment from their joining date, with their base, the tax slab and the payable account.
  9. Create a Leave Policy Assignment for the current leave period.
  10. Preview a payslip and check it against the offer letter.
The first month is prorated

Someone joining on the 10th of a 31-day BS month is paid 22 ÷ 31 of every component marked depends on payment days. Tea is not prorated — it is paid per day actually present, which handles itself.

A leaver

  1. Set Relieving Date on the Employee — this is what stops the pay.
  2. Run the final month's payroll as normal. The last slip prorates to the relieving date.
  3. Check for an outstanding Employee Advance. Anything unrecovered must be settled before the final payment.
  4. Run Yearly Leave Details BS and settle any leave encashment due.
  5. After the final slip is submitted and paid, set Status to Left.
Important

Set the status to Left only after the final payslip. An inactive employee is skipped by the payroll run, so changing the status first means the final month is never paid — and the fix is fiddly.

A transfer between companies

Treat it as a leaver and a joiner. The companies keep separate books, separate holiday lists and separate structures, so one Employee record cannot span both without corrupting the history of each.

Verify a new joiner before their first run

  • Employee list, filtered to them: no blanks in the fields above.
  • One submitted salary assignment, dated from their joining date.
  • Two leave allocations.
  • Attendance rows appearing since their first day.

How income tax is computed

Applies to: Monthly · AutomaticAudience: Accounts, HR Manager
In this articleFrom the month's pay to an annual projection, through the slab, back to a monthly deduction.

Income tax in Nepal is an annual liability paid monthly. The system therefore never taxes a month on its own: it projects the year, applies the slab, and takes what is still owed, spread over the months that remain.

The calculation, step by step

  1. Taxable earnings this month — every earning marked is tax applicable.
  2. Less the exemptions — the SSF deduction is exempt, so it reduces taxable income.
  3. Project the year — what has already been paid this payroll period, plus this month, plus the same again for each month remaining.
  4. Apply the slab to the projected annual figure, band by band. If the employee contributes to the SSF, the 1% band is skipped.
  5. Subtract the tax already deducted in earlier months of the period.
  6. Divide by the months remaining, including this one. That is this month's Income Tax line.
Why a mid-year rise raises tax more than you expect

The projection assumes the new, higher pay for every remaining month, and it also recalculates what the year as a whole should have been. So the first payslip after a rise carries both the higher rate and the catch-up on the months already paid. It is correct, and it settles by the following month.

One-off amounts are taxed in full, that month

A bonus or arrears would otherwise be spread across the remaining months, so the Dashain payslip would show 6 rupees of tax where 70.69 was due. Every generated one-off carries Deduct Full Tax on Selected Payroll Date, and you must tick it on any you type.

The SSF waiver in practice

SSF memberNot an SSF member
First 10,00,000Nil — waived1% (SST)
Above thatIdentical: 10%, 20%, 27%, 29% by band

The waiver is a condition on the slab row reading the employee's SSF Applicable tick. Change that tick and the tax changes from the next slip.

Overriding a person's tax

Tax is computed but changeable. On a draft slip, tick Override Income Tax, type the amount, and record the reason. The override applies to that month only; the annual projection is untouched, so the next month reverts to the computed figure.

Important

An override is an accounting decision with an audit trail attached. Do not use it to make a slip "look right" when the real fault is a wrong base, a missing exemption or bad attendance.

Verify

  1. Pick an SSF member and a non-member on similar pay.
  2. The non-member should carry roughly 1% of the first 10,00,000 more over the year.
  3. Add up one person's Income Tax across all twelve slips; it should match the slab applied to their annual taxable pay.

Calculation reference

Applies to: All companies · FY 83/84Audience: Anyone verifying a figure
In this articleEvery formula the system uses, with worked arithmetic you can check by hand.

Each rule below is stated, then worked through with real numbers. If a payslip disagrees with the arithmetic here, the payslip is built from different inputs — find the input, not the formula.

Proration by payment days

A component marked depends on payment days is scaled by the days paid over the days in the BS month.

amount = monthly figure × payment days ÷ days in month

A monthly allowance of 20,000, 28.5 payment days in a 31-day month:

StepFigure
20,000 × 28.5570,000
÷ 3118,387.10

The divisor is the days in that BS month — 29, 30, 31 or 32 — never a flat 30. Half days contribute 0.5.

Tea & Conveyance

tea = days present × category rate

22 days present at NGI on the OLD rate: 22 × 235 = 5,170.00.

A half day still earns a full day's tea: the person travelled to work. Leave, absence and holidays earn none.

Overtime

hourly basic = Basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8
overtime = approved hours × hourly basic × 1.5

Basic 19,260: 19,260 ÷ 30 = 642.00 a day; ÷ 8 = 80.25 an hour; × 1.5 = 120.38 an overtime hour.

Hours are rounded to the nearest half hour before the multiplication. The divisor of 30 comes from the Labour Act and is deliberately not the BS month length.

Late fine

per-minute rate = Basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8 ÷ 60
fine = late minutes × per-minute rate

Basic 19,260, 38 minutes late across the month: the per-minute rate is 1.2402 and the fine is 38 × 1.2402 = 47.13.

Note

The rate is used unrounded. Rounding it to four decimals first shifted the fine by a paisa — small, but it made the payslip disagree with the report.

There is no grace period, and more than two hours late is a half day as well as a fine. Seven NGI managers are marked Late Fine Exempt and are never fined.

SSF

SSF Addition (earning) = Basic × 20%
SSF (deduction) = Basic × 31%

Both only for employees with SSF Applicable ticked. Neither is prorated by payment days, because Basic already is. The addition appears as an earning so the payslip shows the true cost of employment; the deduction is exempt from income tax.

House Rent Allowance

HRA = Initial Basic × company rate

25% at NGI, 27% at NGN. The base is the Initial Basic — the salary the person started on — not their current basic. Grade-scale companies pay no HRA.

Daily wage

wage = days worked × daily rate

NGK labour at 754.00 a day. A half day counts as half. There is no monthly figure to prorate.

Income tax

See How income tax is computed. In short: project the year, apply the bands, subtract what has already been deducted, divide by the months remaining.

Net pay

gross = every earning, including SSF Addition
deductions = SSF + Income Tax + Late Fine + Salary Advance
net = gross − deductions

The SSF addition raises gross and the SSF deduction lowers net by more, which is why gross moves further than take-home when basic changes.

  4. Recompute forward from there.
  5. Write down what it was. The same fault visits the next company.

Things the system must refuse

Applies to: Test site onlyAudience: QA
In this articleEight attempts that must fail. A test that succeeds here is a defect.

These are as important as the tests that pass. Each guard exists because the mistake it blocks is one a real user makes, and one that is expensive to discover months later.

R1 · Over-allocating leave

Try: allocate 5 more casual days to someone who already has 21.

Must: be refused, naming the person, the ceiling of 21 and the total of 26.

Why: the monthly credit is flexible; the year's total is policy.

R2 · A mid-month pay rise

Try: a Salary Revision effective on the 10th of a BS month.

Must: be refused or clearly warned. A slip is paid at one rate for its whole month.

R3 · Running a revision twice

Try: submit the same revision, same date, again.

Must: be refused — "already has a salary assigned from …".

R4 · Someone else's employee

Try: put an NGN employee on an NGI revision.

Must: be refused, naming the company they belong to.

R5 · A negative payslip

Try: an advance instalment larger than the person's net pay.

Must: be refused — "Grand Total must be >= 0".

R6 · Double proration of SSF

Try: tick depends on payment days on the SSF component and preview a part-month slip.

Must: be refused, because Basic is already prorated.

Then: untick it again before continuing.

R7 · An allowance category from another company

Try: set an NGN tea category on an NGI employee.

Must: not be offered in the list, and be refused if forced.

R8 · A salary journal without its identifiers

Try: save an HR journal with no JV Type or Document No.

Must: be refused. Both are group requirements on every HR journal.

Report a success as a defect

If any of these eight goes through, stop and report it with the exact steps. A guard that is not guarding is worse than no guard, because everyone downstream believes it is holding.
