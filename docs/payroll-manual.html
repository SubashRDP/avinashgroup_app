1.1 · What payroll must produce, every month

Three things, in this order. Everything in this manual exists to make one of them correct.

  1. A payslip for each person — what they earned, what was deducted, what they take home. It must be defensible line by line, because people check their own pay.
  2. One accounting entry — the company's salary cost in the books, split by section, with what is owed to the Social Security Fund and to the tax office shown separately.
  3. A payment — the money leaving the bank, matching the payslips to the rupee.

Before this system, all three came from an Excel sheet typed each month. The sheet is still the reference: where this manual gives a number, it is a number the sheet also produces.

The one idea to hold on to

Pay is not typed. It is derived — from what the person is entitled to (their record), and what actually happened (their attendance). If a payslip is wrong, one of those two is wrong, and this manual tells you which field to look at.

1.2 · The chain, end to end

Money reaches a payslip along one path. Learn this and the rest of the manual is detail.

Fingerprint punch→Attendance→Allowance engine→Additional Salary→Salary Slip→Journal Entry→Bank payment
StepWhat happensDecided by
PunchThe machine records a time against a device IDThe ID on the Employee
AttendanceEach hour, the punches for a day become one row: Present, Half Day or Absent, with late minutes measuredThe shift the person was on that day
Allowance engineOnce a month, counts days and hours and turns them into moneyTheir allowance category and employee category
Additional SalaryOne record per person per allowance, saying qty × rate = amountWritten by the engine, not by hand
Salary SlipFixed pay from the structure, prorated by days paid, plus those extras, minus SSF and taxThe salary structure and their Basic
Journal EntryThe total cost posted to the books, split by cost centreComponent → account mapping and the employee's cost centre

Two halves. Fixed pay (Basic, house rent, gas, education, dearness) lives in the salary structure and changes rarely. Variable pay (tea, meals, overtime, late fines, advances) never touches the structure — it is worked out from attendance each month and attached to that month only. That separation is why a rate change is one edit and why last month's payslip never changes.

1.3 · Why everything is in Bikram Sambat

The company's pay month is a Nepali month — Shrawan, Bhadra, Ashwin — not January or February. A BS month starts mid-way through an English one: Bhadra 2083 runs from 17 August to 16 September 2026.

This is not cosmetic. It decides:

  • Total working days on a payslip — 31 for Bhadra, because Bhadra has 31 days.
  • When leave is credited — 2.75 days on the last day of each BS month.
  • Which month an allowance belongs to — a meal earned on 20 August is Bhadra's, not August's.
  • The fiscal year — Shrawan 1 to Ashadh end (2026-07-17 → 2027-07-16 for 83/84). Leave, tax and salary all run on that year.

You do not convert dates yourself. On a Payroll Entry you type a posting date inside the month you are paying, and the start and end dates snap to that BS month by themselves. Reports ask for a BS month by name.

What goes wrong if this is off

If the BS setting is disabled, a payslip covers an English month: 31 total days but only 25 of them counted as paid, so a perfect month pays 25/31 of the salary. That exact fault cost every employee a fifth of their pay before it was found.

Two pay models, because the companies grew differently. Initial-basic companies pay allowances as percentages and flat amounts tied to a grade figure. The cutoff field, and why it is called out twice

Saving a Shift Type once left this field holding the clock time of the save — 08:29 — which nobody typed and nobody saw. Because the rule takes the earlier of the two cutoffs, every person arriving after 08:29 became a half day: 2,423 of 2,808 days in one test month, which in real money is roughly half the month's wages for everyone. The rule now ignores a cutoff at or before the shift start, but whenever you open a Shift Type, check that this field is empty.

Shifts are per company and named with the company: NGI 9 AM - 6 PM, NGN 7 AM - 3 PM. That way Karnali's hours can change without touching Narayani's.

2.13 · Cost centres and accounts

The chart of accounts splits salary cost three ways — 547101 office, 547102 sales and distribution, 547103 filling plant. But a salary component maps to one account per company, so the account alone cannot tell a plant wage from an office wage.

How payment days become money

A payslip's fixed pay is prorated: amount × payment days ÷ total working days.

Worked example

Bhadra has 31 days. Someone with 2 absences and 1 half day has 28.5 payment days. On a Basic of 20,000 that is 20,000 × 28.5 ÷ 31 = 18,387.10. House rent, gas, education and dearness are cut in the same proportion. SSF, being 31% of the already-prorated Basic, follows without being prorated again.

3.2 · Tea and meals

Tea & conveyance

Rule: days present × the rate of their allowance group. A half day earns the full day's tea — the client's position is that turning up earns it.

Worked example

NGI OLD, 22 days present including one half day: 22 × 235 = 5,170. The same attendance in NGI NEW: 22 × 40 = 880. In NGI NO: nothing.

Meals

Rule: one meal for coming 1½ hours early, one for staying 1½ hours late, never more than two in a day. On a holiday it goes by hours worked: 6 hours earns one, 8 hours earns two.

Worked example

9 AM shift. IN 07:20 (1 h 40 m early) and OUT 18:45 (1 h 45 m late) = 2 meals = 150 at NGI's rate of 75. IN 08:10 and OUT 17:30 = 0 meals — neither side reaches 1½ hours.

Why it is measured this way: counting one meal per present day paid 132,525 in a test month against the sheet's 52,275. Meals are earned by the extra hours, not by attending.

3.3 · Overtime and the late fine

Overtime

Two conditions, both required: the person is Operation, and the day is on a submitted Overtime Sheet. Hours come from the punches, never from typing.

DayHours counted
Working dayonly the hours before the shift starts and after it ends
Holidayevery hour worked
Roundingto the nearest half hour — 7:53 → 8.0, 7:34 → 7.5, 5:59 → 6.0

Rate: Basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8 × 1.5 — the Labour Act's hourly wage at time and a half.

Worked example

Basic 19,260 → 19,260 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 = 80.25 an hour → × 1.5 = 120.38. Nine authorised hours = 1,083.38. That is exactly what the client's sheet pays.

Officers instead earn leave: an Admin & Officer employee on an Overtime Sheet shows Replacement Leave, gets no money, and the day is credited back in Yearly Leave Details.

Late fine

Rule: late minutes × Basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8 ÷ 60 — the same hourly wage, per minute. "Late time" means arriving late plus leaving early, as the client's own sheet adds them.

Worked example

38 minutes late on a Basic of 17,860: 17,860 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 ÷ 60 = 1.2402 a minute → 38 × 1.2402 = 47.13.

Two exceptions: a day already cut to a half day is not fined, and anyone with Late Fine Exempt ticked is never fined — the client's sheet types a zero rate for its managers.

3.4 · SSF, and how tax is worked out

The Social Security Fund

For a member, on a Basic of 20,000:

LineAmountMeaning
SSF Addition (earning)+4,000the company's 20% share, added to pay so it can be deducted with the rest
SSF (deduction)−6,20031% — the company's 20% plus the employee's 11%
Effect on take-home−2,200the employee's own 11%
Effect on tax−6,200 of taxable paythe whole contribution is exempt

Income tax, step by step

  1. Take this month's taxable pay and project the year: months already paid + this month × the months remaining.
  2. Subtract the SSF contribution for the year — that is what Allow Tax Exemption does.
  3. Read the yearly tax off the slab. If the person is in the SSF, the 1% band is skipped entirely.
  4. Subtract tax already deducted this year, and spread what is left over the months remaining.
  5. Extra pay — overtime, bonus, arrears — is taxed in the month it is paid, not spread.
Worked example — the two kinds of person

In the SSF, earning 25,000 a month: the year comes to 300,000, under ten lakh, and the 1% band does not apply. Tax = 0.
Outside the SSF, earning 15,000 a month: the year is 180,000, the 1% band applies. Tax = 1,800 a year, about 150 a month — which is exactly what the client's sheet shows against that name.

Overriding: every draft slip carries Override Income Tax and an amount, with the computed figure shown beside it. Typing 0 means no tax this month. The projection is only an estimate of a year that has not happened; accounts often knows better.

3.5 · Leave arithmetic

Earned monthly, not granted yearly. The year's entitlement is divided by twelve and credited on the last day of each BS month.

CompanyCasualSickPer BS month
NGI, NGN, NGG and the rest21121.75 + 1.00 = 2.75
Karnali (NGK)8120.67 + 1.00 = 1.67
Probation12—1.00

The month's amount is flexible, the year's total is not. An admin may grant three days one month and four the next — but the year can never exceed 21 (or 8 at Karnali). The system refuses the excess and names the figure.

Rounding 0.25 means a part month is credited to the nearest quarter day, so a mid-month joiner earns a fair fraction rather than nothing or everything.

The sandwich rule (Include Holiday ticked): leave on Friday and Sunday makes Saturday leave too.

Unpaid leave reduces payment days, and therefore Basic, the allowances and the SSF with them.

4.1 · Every day

  1. Open Monthly Attendance BS for the current BS month. Why: the machine has already built yesterday's attendance; your job is to catch what it could not.
  2. Look for three things: "Not Marked" days, an Absent for someone who was clearly at work, and an IN with no OUT. Each means a punch was missed.
  3. Fix a day — open Attendance for that person and date. A draft can be edited; a submitted row must be Cancelled, then Amended. Say why in the remarks. Why: pay follows this row, and someone will ask about it in six months.
  4. After a machine outage, use Attendance Fix for the shift and date range instead of fixing rows one by one. It builds attendance from punches, marks true absences, skips holidays, and never overwrites what a person entered by hand.
Do not be kind

Marking someone Present who was not there pays tea, a full day's Basic and possibly a meal. It is faster to fix the punch.

4.2 · The monthly run, step by step

Each step says what it does and how you know it worked. The order is not optional.

  1. Check attendance is complete. Monthly Attendance BS for the month, no "Not Marked". Why: everything below is derived from it, and a fix afterwards means redoing slips.
  2. Submit and measure the Overtime Sheets. Each sheet: Submit, then Measure from Attendance. Why: unmeasured sheets pay nothing.
  3. Enter the extras. Payroll Adjustment for one-off amounts, Dashain Bonus in its month, Salary Revision for raises, and pay out any new advance. Why: they must exist before slips are built.
  4. New Payroll Entry — Company, Payroll Frequency Monthly, a Posting Date inside the month. The BS dates fill themselves. Get Employees, then Save. Do not submit yet. Check: the employee count matches your active staff with a salary.
  5. Nepal HRMS → Prepare Payroll Inputs. What it does: reads the month's attendance and writes tea, meals, authorised overtime, late fines, daily wages and advance instalments as Additional Salary. Check: the message reports how many rows. Safe to press again — it replaces, never doubles.
  6. Create Salary Slips. This submits the Payroll Entry and builds one draft slip per person. Check: the slip count equals the employee count.
  7. Check the slips. Open five. Verify payment days against the attendance, and check one person's tea and overtime with the arithmetic in section 3. Look hard at Income Tax; override where accounts says so.
  8. Submit Salary Slip. What it does: finalises every slip and writes one Journal Entry. Check: debit equals credit, and Salary Payable equals the month's net pay.
  9. Make Bank Entry when the money is paid. What it does: clears Salary Payable against the bank.
If you skip step 5

The slips come out with no tea and no meals. Recovery: run Prepare Payroll Inputs, then open each affected slip and press Save so it picks the amounts up. Easier to do it in order.

More than 30 employees

Slip creation is handed to a background worker. If the queue is not running, the Payroll Entry sits at status Queued and no slips appear — that is an infrastructure problem, not a payroll one.

  2. Run in order. Setup cases first — if one fails, later cases fail for the wrong reason.
  3. A case passes only if every line under Expect is true. Partly right is a fail.

6.1 · When something is wrong

Somebody is missing from the payroll run

Cause: no submitted Salary Structure Assignment, or one with no Payroll Payable Account, or it starts after this month.

Fix: create or correct it, then Get Employees again.

A slip shows very few paid days

Cause: attendance is missing or marked Absent — often a wrong or blank Attendance Device ID, so their punches matched nobody.

Fix: correct the device ID, rebuild the days with Attendance Fix, rerun Prepare Payroll Inputs, then save the slip.

No tea or meals on any slip

Cause: slips were created before Prepare Payroll Inputs.

Fix: run the inputs, then open each slip and Save.

No tea for one person only

Cause: their Allowance Category is empty, or it is the NO group.

Everybody is on Half Day

Cause: the shift's Late Arrival Cutoff holds a stray time.

Fix: clear it, then rebuild the month's attendance.

Overtime is not paid

Cause: no submitted Overtime Sheet, the sheet was not measured, or the person is Admin & Officer (who earn leave instead).

A manager was fined for lateness

Fix: tick Late Fine Exempt and rerun the inputs.

Submitting slips fails with an account error

Cause: a component has no Accounts row for that company, or the payroll payable account has an account type set.

Fix: accountant maps the component; clear the account type on the payable account.

The Payroll Entry says "Queued" and no slips appear

Cause: runs over 30 people hand slip creation to a background worker, and the worker is not running.

Fix: ask IT to start the queue, then Create Salary Slips again.

Tax looks too small

Cause: the projection assumes the rest of the year looks like this month; a new joiner's first month therefore projects a small year.

Fix: override the figure on the slip if accounts knows the real number.

6.2 · Known limits — by design, not defects

  • Auto attendance skips holidays. Holiday work appears only after Attendance Fix.
  • A slip is paid at one rate for the whole month. A rise dated mid-month takes effect the following month; use arrears for the gap.
  • Unmarked days are paid under the current Payroll Settings.
  • Runs over 30 people queue slip creation to a background worker.
  • Tea, meals and overtime are not in the structure — they belong to one month only.
  • Employee fields are current, not historical. Changing a group today does not alter a submitted slip, but it changes the next run.
  • A component maps to one account per company — the office/sales/plant split comes from cost centres, not accounts.
Reporting a problem

Give the case number, the record name, what you expected, what you saw, and the figures. A slip or journal name is enough for anyone to reproduce it.
